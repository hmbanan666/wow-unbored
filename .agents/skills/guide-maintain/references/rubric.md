# Editorial review: pass or repair

Apply each dimension to the actual draft. A failed accuracy, intent or usability check blocks publication; polish cannot compensate. Do not average the rows into an SEO score.

| Dimension | Pass evidence | Repair signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Promise | Title, description and first paragraph answer the same specific question | A "leveling guide" with no route, or solo and leveling pages with interchangeable openings |
| Decision | Reader can choose, name the tradeoff and take a next action | A ranking restated as paragraphs; "choose what you enjoy" with no way to compare |
| Truth | Consequential claims trace to game/build-specific evidence; inference is recognizable as advice | Repeated secondary paraphrases treated as confirmation, beta numbers presented as live, invented play/testing |
| Interest | Concrete friction, a revealing comparison or a useful example earns every section | Source-by-source summary, abstract claims of versatility, paragraphs that only say to be careful |
| Clarity | Essential meaning is in prose; unfamiliar terms have short explanations | Acronyms, bare skill names or hover-only prerequisites |
| Uncertainty | A scoped evidence note plus local labels only for distinct disputed claims | "Early forecast" repeated in every class entry, or uncertainty removed to sound decisive |
| Navigation/search | Unique descriptive metadata, useful headings, intent-matched links, valid canonical and indexability | Keyword variants as headings, irrelevant cross-links, hidden FAQs, fake review/rating schema |
| Maintenance | New version, actual access dates, source builds and explicit update triggers | Publication date passed off as a live test or a scheduled date bump without changed content |

## Banned patterns and repair actions

Treat these as revision failures, not a keyword filter. A necessary factual distinction can stay; repeated rhetorical contrasts cannot.

- **Self-referential hedges:** "This guide cannot establish…", "These examples explain how to read it", "This uncertainty belongs to…". Delete the commentary. State the actual limitation once beside the affected claim: "The new gear's binding restrictions remain unconfirmed in the beta tooltips."
- **Stacked contrasts:** repeated "not X, but Y", "rather than", "not a promise", "not proof" or "different things". Write the positive action and its reason. Keep a contrast only when it prevents a likely mistake, such as looking for Disenchant in the spellbook instead of the Professions window. Flag a second contrast in the same paragraph and review every survivor aloud.
- **Filler:** "It is important to note", "in this guide", "a clear demand story", "a concrete way to choose", "choose what you enjoy" without a tradeoff, or a paragraph that only repeats the heading. Replace it with a mechanic, consequence or action; cut it when nothing is lost.
- **Repeated evidence disclaimers:** repeated "early forecast", "check current data" or "no guarantee" without a distinct uncertain fact. Put the scope beside the opening recommendation; qualify only separate unresolved mechanics locally.
- **Invented authority:** personal anecdotes, test results, prices or certainty absent from the evidence ledger. Remove the claim or reopen research. Confident wording cannot repair missing evidence.
- **Em dashes in player copy:** use a period, comma or colon. Keep sentences short enough that punctuation does not need to carry a second argument.

Before passing, mark each section's useful fact or decision, delete sentences about the writing itself, and count contrast constructions. Reduce them to the few that prevent a real misunderstanding; do not replace them mechanically with synonyms.

## Search and image checks

- Name one **target query** and the reader's intent in the brief. Compare neighboring guides so each URL answers a distinct question. Use the query naturally in the title and opening; headings should describe decisions, not repeat keyword variants.
- Aim for a **title of 45–65 characters** and a **description of 120–160 characters**, including spaces. These are editorial targets, not search-engine limits or ranking factors. Front-load the subject and deciding benefit. Read the actual rendered title, including any site suffix, and shorten if it obscures the promise. Allow a reasoned exception when accuracy needs it; never pad to meet a count.
- Give every page a unique title and description matching the answer it delivers. Preserve the existing public URL; verify canonical, indexability, sitemap inclusion and HTTP status through the publication flow's technical audit.
- Write **alt text** for meaningful images that describes what is visibly shown and why it matters here. Use the verified location or item name only when known. Decorative images need empty alt text where the renderer supports it. Avoid keyword lists, "image of", invented context and promises the image cannot substantiate. Follow the existing media rules; alt text is not a reason to add an image.

## Good/bad transformations

**Ranking → decision**

Bad: "Affliction is S tier. Protection is A tier, close to S. Both are excellent solo choices."

Better: "Start with Affliction if you want damage to keep working while you recover resources. Prefer Protection if standing your ground matters more than fast kills: its defenses buy safety, but repeated Consecration costs mana." Use only after verifying these mechanics; the distinction is the lesson, not reusable game truth.

**Caveat → bounded claim**

Bad: "This is an early forecast. The early forecast favors Hunter. Remember that the forecast is early."

Better: Put "The comparison uses pre-launch client data, not timed 1–60 runs" once beside the answer. Then explain Hunter's petless opening and later pet management. The uncertainty qualifies the comparison; it need not swallow it.

**Fact dump → consequence**

Bad: "The profession has 272 abilities and 96 reagent changes."

Better: "Before buying a stack of materials, open the recipe you intend to learn and check its current ingredients. A Classic shopping list can send you after the wrong materials." Keep the underlying change evidence in the ledger.

**Fake precision → real calculation**

Bad: "Farm this route for 50 gold per hour."

Better: "Compare the crate's silver reward with what its required materials could sell for. If you want Merchant's Favor for a recipe, treat the lost gold as the cost of that unlock." This is a decision rule, not a fabricated price example.

**Decorative explanation → useful explanation**

Bad: "Life Tap: a Warlock ability."

Better: "Life Tap trades health for mana. It keeps casting going, but using it while taking damage reduces your safety margin." Verify it in the correct Game; do not silently attach a Retail spell id.

## Read-through

Read once as a newcomer with no source tabs open, then once as an expert looking for an unsupported leap. For each section ask: what did I learn, what can I now decide, and which sentence supplies the evidence or limitation? If the only answer is "another site says so," synthesize the mechanics and their consequence or cut the section.
